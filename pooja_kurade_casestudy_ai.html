01 The brief

The work existed. It was just unreadable.

My user here is a hiring manager with a stack of applications and a few minutes each. That is the whole brief. Everything else follows from it.

What I had instead was three Figma boards. Each one designed carefully, each one a different accent colour and a different idea of what a case study contains, and all of them living in a file that required a link, a login and forty minutes of scrolling.

Where I started, three Figma boards

Presentable one at a time, in a meeting, with me talking over them. Useless to someone deciding on their own in four minutes.

The test I wrote down first

Skimmable in two minutes. Worth reading for twenty.

Two audiences, one page. A recruiter scanning for relevance and a design lead looking for reasoning. Every structural decision on this site is that sentence being applied.

03 Decisions

Four calls, and what each one cost

All four serve the same user in the same four minutes. Three of them are subtractions.

Decision 01 · Consistency

One shell for five pages, not five designs

Options
Let each case study look like its project, or hold one layout and let the content differ.
I chose
One shell. Identical header, section rhythm, type scale and palette on every page. The client's colour appears in the banner only.
Why
Someone reading a second case study should spend their attention on the work, not on relearning where things are. Familiarity is a reading speed feature.
Cost
The pages are less individually striking than my old boards were. I traded surprise for speed, deliberately.
The drift I had to catch
#2FBE8E homepage
#3DDC7A status dot
#6EDBB8 a badge

Three greens, each plausible on its own page. Every page was built in its own conversation, so consistency was never going to arrive by itself. I audited all five for stray hex values, then wrote down what each colour is allowed to mean.

Decision 02 · Content

Decisions with their cost, not a wall of screens

Options
Show everything I made with captions, or show the forks where I chose one thing over another.
I chose
Three or four decisions per case study, each framed as options, what I chose, why, and what it cost.
Why
Screens prove I can use Figma. The cost line proves I understood the tradeoff, and it is the part interviewers actually probe.
Cost
A lot of finished visual work is not on the site. It also invites harder questions, which I decided was the point.

Decision 03 · Direction

Print that happens to scroll

Problem
I knew what I did not want and could not name what I did. A dozen adjectives got me a dozen different pages.
I chose
Reference sites instead of adjectives, then one phrase I could repeat: warm, editorial, print that happens to scroll.
Why
A reference is not a thing to copy, it is a shared vocabulary. It also matches the brief: long-form reading wants paper, not a product page.
Cost
It is a quiet site. It will not win anyone over in a thumbnail, which is a real tradeoff for a portfolio.
Ruled out
✕ Gradient hero, oversized headline
✕ Cards floating on a dark blur
✕ Big numbers with no source
Kept
→ Warm paper, not white
→ Long measure, generous leading
→ Rules and labels doing the dividing
Decision 04 · Craft

Hold the details nothing else was watching

Problem
A page can be locally correct and globally wrong. Nothing in the build process was looking at the whole.
I chose
To treat every page as a proof against one written system, and to re-audit all five whenever anything changed.
Caught
Body copy arriving at 15, 15.5 and 16.5px on three pages. Panels capped at a readable text width, so the container shrank with its paragraph and broke the column edge. Four uneven cards in a rigid two by two.
Cost
Slow, unglamorous, and most of the hours on this project. It is also the only reason five pages read as one site.

05 Outcome

It exists, and it passes its own test

5
Pages live, from three unbuilt Figma boards
1
Palette, type scale and layout across all of them
0
Numbers on this site I cannot account for
Being straight about it

I have no analytics to quote and no interview data on my own portfolio. What I can say is that it shipped after two years of not shipping, it holds one system across five pages, and I can defend every decision on it without reaching. For a self-directed project that is the honest measure.

What I would carry into a team

Execution stopped being the constraint

Trying a layout used to cost an afternoon, so I tried one. Here it cost a sentence, so I tried four and chose. What is scarce now is knowing which of the four is right.

Everything got faster except deciding.

Nothing else was looking at the whole

Each page came back locally excellent and globally adrift. Systems thinking was the entire contribution, and it is the difference between five pages and one site.

Consistency is a decision, not an output.

Editing became the job

When a section costs nothing to add, restraint is the only thing keeping a portfolio readable. Most of my instructions were deletions.

Cheap to add is not worth adding.

The tool built what I designed. Deciding what belonged, and noticing when it drifted, was the work.
