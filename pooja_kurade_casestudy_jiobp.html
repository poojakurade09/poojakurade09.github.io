01 The brief

What the client actually asked for

Refuelling in India is unpredictable. You drive to a station, join whatever queue you find, and you have no idea about wait times or the price until you are already there. JIO-BP saw a chance to move that decision onto a phone.

The phase one requirement was specific. A customer books fuel in the app, drives to the station, fills up, and collects reward points for doing it that way. Digitise the booking, take pressure off the forecourt at peak hours, and start a loyalty relationship with someone who buys fuel every week.

Phase one scope, agreed with the client
01 Find a station nearby and see what it offers before setting off
02 Book fuel in advance, then complete it at the pump
03 Earn and see reward points without hunting for them
04 Give fleet owners visibility and control over vehicles and spend

Worth saying plainly: this was a concept sprint before build, so I worked from the client's requirements, stakeholder conversations and a review of competitor apps. I did not get access to real drivers or fleet owners, and nothing here was validated with users. That shaped how I designed and it is the first thing I would change.

02 Two users

One app, two jobs that barely overlap

This was the real design problem. A commuter opens the app for two minutes to hold a slot. A fleet owner opens it to run part of a business. Same login, same brand, very different reasons to be there.

User one
The everyday driver

Fills up on the way somewhere. Wants to know a station is worth the detour, hold a slot, pay, and get on with the day. Rewards are a reason to keep using the app rather than the reason to open it.

What the app owes them
Nearby stations and what each one offers, on one screen
A booking that visibly holds, so there is no doubt on the drive over
Points that turn up on their own
User two
The fleet owner

Runs many vehicles and many drivers. Cannot see who fuelled where, cannot reconcile spend, and is not going to hand over one large payment on trust. Needs the app to be a record, not just a booking tool.

What the app owes them
Every vehicle and every transaction in one place
Spend they can check per vehicle, not one lump sum
History they can hand to accounts without rebuilding it
How I resolved it

One shared booking flow, because the act of reserving fuel is identical for both. Then a dashboard that changes weight depending on who you are: a driver sees their last booking and their points, a fleet owner sees vehicles, spend and transaction history. No separate app, no mode switch to explain.

04 Decisions

Three calls, and what each one cost

Everything else was execution. These are the forks where I picked one thing over another, on reasoning rather than data, which means I can tell you exactly what I was betting on.

Decision 01 · Finding a station

A map, not a ranked list

Options
A list of nearby stations sorted by distance, or a map with station cards on top of it.
I chose
The map, with fuel type and price on the card so nobody has to drill in to compare.
Why
Fuelling is a detour decision. Whether a station is on your way matters more than whether it is closest, and a list cannot show that. Search is there for the driver who already knows where they are going.
Cost
A map is heavier to build, slower on a weak signal, and less useful to a fleet owner booking for someone else. It is also the assumption I would test first.
Nearby, with price on the card
Decision 02 · Trust

The booking stays visible after you book it

Options
Confirm and dismiss, or hold the booking as a state you can come back to.
I chose
A confirmation with the booking details kept on the screen, and the same booking waiting on the dashboard.
Why
You are being asked to commit money before you get anything. The gap between booking and arriving at the pump is where doubt lives, and a message that disappears cannot answer a question you have ten minutes later in the car.
Cost
More state for engineering to maintain, and an expired booking becomes a real screen someone has to design and handle.
The booking stays reachable
Decision 03 · Serving both users

The dashboard carries the difference

Tension
A driver wants two minutes and a held slot. A fleet owner wants vehicles, spend and history. Both arrive at the same home screen.
I chose
Shared booking, split dashboard. Rewards and last booking for a driver, vehicles and transaction history for a fleet owner.
Also
Fleet owners were clear that they would not hand over one large amount up front, so spend is shown per vehicle and per transaction rather than as a single balance.
Cost
Rewards get quieter for the driver who cares about them, and the fleet view is only as good as the data the stations actually send back.

07 What comes next

What I would test if this went further

This is a designed concept, not a validated product. If phase two happened, these are the three things I would put in front of real people first, in this order.

1
Will anyone pay before they arrive

The whole model rests on pre payment. I would take the booking flow to a forecourt and watch where people stop, because if trust breaks there, nothing else in the app matters.

2
Map or list, settled properly

I chose the map on reasoning. That deserves a real comparison with a handful of drivers on both, including someone on a poor connection at a station.

3
Whether one app really serves both users

The shared booking and split dashboard is my central bet. Two or three fleet owners walking through their actual week would tell me quickly whether it holds or whether they need their own product.

What I would do differently

Push harder for user access

Even two conversations at a station in week one would have grounded this. I accepted the constraint faster than I should have.

Design the unhappy paths sooner

Expired bookings, failed payments, no signal at the pump. These came late, and they are exactly where a fuel app gets judged.

Bring the fleet user in earlier

They arrived mid brief and got the second half of my attention. A second user changes the information architecture, not just a screen.
